# First-time how-to for new staff (template and wording)

200 words or fewer, in one copy box. Plain words, one action per line, written to the staff member ("you"). Use the owner's steps and window. The AI can't see the owner's billing software, so every software step uses `[menu name in your billing software]` and a line telling the staff member to ask the owner to show it once.

## Template
```
Monthly [job]: how to do it (first time)
Near expiry here means: [window] or less before the expiry date.

- Get the tracker sheet, a pen and the "Set aside" box.
- Ask [owner] to show you each step once.

Steps
1. Start at [first shelf]. Go shelf by shelf, then the storeroom.
2. Read the expiry date on each pack. Write expired and near-expiry items in the tracker: item, batch, expiry date, quantity.
3. Put those items in the "Set aside: owner to decide" box.
4. Give the list to [owner].
5. Open [menu name in your billing software] and make one purchase return per supplier. Write its number in the tracker.
6. Pack each supplier's items in one labeled box with a copy of the purchase return.
7. At pickup, get your copy signed or stamped. Write the date in the tracker.

What to check
- The expiry or "use by" date. If a pack shows only a packing date and "best before", ask [owner].
- Quantity in the box matches the purchase return.

Common mistakes
- Skipping the storeroom or the back of the shelf.
- Putting set-aside items back on the shelf.
- Packing without the purchase return copy.

Not sure? Stop and ask [owner].
```

## Notes
- Cut steps the owner didn't ask about (for example, no billing step if they only asked for the count).
- Mistakes are about the task, never about a person.
- Never add anything about whether expired items are safe, how to store medicines or how to throw anything away. If the owner asks, say: "check with a qualified person and your supplier's terms."
- If the owner writes in Hindi, Marathi, Konkani or Hinglish, write the how-to in that language, keep the bracket placeholders, and end the reply with the reads-it-well line.
