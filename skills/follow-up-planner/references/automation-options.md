# Automation options: follow-up-planner

Owners often ask for "a bot that follows up for me". This page says plainly what each option can and cannot do. Claude in this helper drafts messages and does the date math. It cannot send, schedule or read WhatsApp or email. The owner sends.

Facts about apps change. Menu names and limits can differ by phone and over time, so check inside the app.

## 1. WhatsApp Business app (free, on your phone): what you can do today

- **Broadcast lists.** You write one message and send it to a list. Each person gets it as a private message, and replies come back to you one by one. Two limits matter:
  - It only reaches people who have **saved your number** in their phone. Others do not get it, and you are not told who missed it.
  - **You send each broadcast yourself.** It does not follow up on its own, and it does not know who replied.
  - There is a limit on how many people one list can hold. Check the current limit in the app.
- **Labels.** Tag each chat, for example "Step 1 sent", "Step 2 sent", "Step 3 sent", "Replied", "Do not contact". Then filter your chats by label to see who is where.
- **Quick replies.** Save each step's message once (for example shortcuts `fu-first`, `fu-second`, `fu-last`). In any chat, type / and the shortcut to send it in two taps.
- **Greeting and away messages.** These answer people who write to you. They do not follow up people who went quiet.

A good daily routine with the free tools:
1. Open your tracker's "Due today" tab (see SKILL.md, Tracker sheet).
2. For each code due, open that chat, type / and the shortcut, check it, send.
3. Change the chat label and update Step and Last message date in your sheet.
4. At the end of the day, type short notes and ask this helper for the report.

A daily Google Calendar reminder to yourself ("Follow-ups: check Due today") at a time you choose helps you keep the habit.

## 2. Truly automatic WhatsApp follow-ups: a builder project

Sending follow-ups automatically, and stopping when someone replies, needs the **WhatsApp Business Platform** (not the free app), usually set up through an **approved provider**. Before anything is built, you would need:

- **Opt-in.** Each person must have agreed to get WhatsApp messages from your group. Keep a record of how and when they agreed.
- **Approved message templates.** Messages your group starts (like a follow-up after silence) must use templates that WhatsApp has approved in advance. You cannot send any text you like.
- **Per-message costs.** Messages your business starts are charged. Prices depend on the kind of message and the country, and they change, so get current prices in writing before you start.
- **Consent and do-not-disturb rules.** India has rules on promotional messages and calls, and on using people's personal data. Check with a qualified person what applies to your group.
- **A way to stop.** If someone replies "stop", the system must stop at once and never message them again.
- **Someone to read replies.** A bot can send, but a person still answers real replies.
- **Your number.** Moving your number to the Platform can change how you use it in the phone app. Ask before you switch.

A builder can help set this up. Ask them to keep names and numbers in your group's own system, not in an AI chat.

No provider is recommended here. Compare a few, and ask each for costs, how opt-out works and where your data is kept.

## 3. Email follow-ups

- **Gmail scheduled send.** Write the follow-up, then tap the arrow or ⋮ next to Send and choose **Schedule send**. You pick the day and time. You schedule each email yourself, and Gmail does not stop a scheduled email if the person replies first, so check before it goes.
- **Gmail templates** (on a computer, in Settings > Advanced) let you save each step's text, like quick replies.
- Email tools that send sequences automatically exist; they also need people's consent and a way to unsubscribe. A builder can help choose and set one up.

## 4. What never to do, with any tool

- Buy or copy a list of numbers or emails and message them. It breaks people's trust, can get your number blocked, and may break the law.
- Message someone's family, friends or another number to reach them.
- Keep messaging after a no, a "stop" or step 3.
